Why block storage does not compare like object storage

  • You pay for what you provision, not what you store. Every vendor here bills the allocated size of the volume, empty or full. Object storage bills the bytes actually held.
  • Performance is a separate line item at AWS gp3 and io2, Azure Premium SSD v2 and Ultra, Google Hyperdisk and STACKIT. Everyone else folds it into the capacity rate, and caps it.
  • Azure, uniquely, sells fixed disk sizes. A 600 GiB Premium SSD disk is billed as a 1,024 GiB P30. Change the volume count above and watch that rounding move the row.
  • Snapshots are not free and are rarely priced like the volume. Exoscale charges the volume rate; AWS and Google charge roughly half; Hetzner does not offer them at all.

Who states their unit, and who doesn't

  • Binary, stated in writing — AWS, Azure (meters read “1 GiB/Hour”), Google (“1 gibibyte month”), Exoscale (per GiB·hour), T Cloud Public (“1 GB = 1,073,741,824 Byte”).
  • Not stated — OVHcloud, Scaleway, Hetzner, IONOS, Infomaniak, STACKIT. Those rows carry a marker and follow the selector at the top of the page. The gap between the two assumptions is 7.4 %.
  • OVHcloud prints GiB for object storage but GB for block storage, without defining either.
  • The storage medium under each class is the vendor's own words. The filter groups them into HDD & magnetic, SSD, and Premium SSD — the last being each vendor's own top-performance flash tier, not a judgement about the hardware. Google's Hyperdisk Throughput sits with the HDDs because Google itself compares its latency to a hard disk. STACKIT states no medium anywhere and gets its own bucket.

Regions and redundancy are not interchangeable

  • AWS EBS and Azure managed disks are single-zone by default. Azure ZRS and Google Regional PD or Hyperdisk Balanced HA are the multi-zone rows, and they cost 1.5× to 2×.
  • OVHcloud charges the same rate for its 3-AZ Regional Classic Volume as for the single-zone Classic Volume — the only vendor here that does not price zone redundancy separately.
  • Hetzner, Infomaniak and Exoscale keep several copies inside one zone; Hetzner says three physical servers, Infomaniak three Ceph replicas.
  • Scaleway, IONOS and STACKIT publish no redundancy model for block storage at all.

Documented gaps, left as gaps

  • T Cloud Public (formerly Open Telekom Cloud) publishes no EVS rate card. Its pricing-model page uses 4.6 ct/GB-month in a worked example; that is an illustration, not a price list, so the row carries no figure.
  • Azure snapshots are the most contested field on this page. The retail feed carries $0.145/GiB-month against Premium SSD, $0.05 against Standard HDD, and hourly instant-access meters for Premium SSD v2 and Ultra. Incremental snapshots land on standard storage, so $0.05 is used for the P, E and S rows.
  • IONOS shows €0.04/GB/30 d for Essential on the German page and $0.0533 on the US page — a gap FX does not explain, the same pattern as its object storage pricing.
  • STACKIT lists every SKU twice, with and without an -m suffix at roughly double the price. The suffix is never defined; the non--m EU01 rates are used.
  • Hetzner ranks first partly because its row carries no snapshot cost — it offers none for volumes. That is a missing capability, not a discount.
  • Wasabi, Backblaze and Cloudflare sell no block storage and are absent from this page by design.

Scenario assumptions

  • The capacity, IOPS and throughput figures are per volume; the provisioned total is split evenly across the volume count. That matters because free IOPS baselines and Azure's size ladder are per disk.
  • The chart's Capacity only toggle drops provisioned IOPS, throughput and snapshots. It is there to separate the shelf price from the bill: STACKIT is second cheapest on capacity and ninth once its per-disk performance class is added, and AWS io2 moves further still.
  • Rows whose published ceiling cannot deliver the requested IOPS, throughput or volume size are marked ⚠ and their cost is what the vendor would charge for the largest configuration they do offer.
  • Per-I/O charges — Azure Standard SSD and HDD transactions, AWS magnetic — are shown but not monetised: both need an I/O profile specific to the workload. Rows carrying one are marked +io.
  • Attachment limits, multi-attach, encryption keys, cross-region replication and Ultra Disk's per-vCPU reservation are shown in the notes but not costed.
  • Prices exclude VAT, negotiated discounts and commitment terms.

How the rates were collected

Every figure comes from the vendor's own price feed where one exists, so nothing depends on reading a rendered table:

  • AWS — the Price List Bulk API, AmazonEC2 offer for eu-central-1, product families Storage, System Operation and Provisioned Throughput, publication 9 September 2026.
  • Azure — the Retail Prices API, armRegionName eq 'germanywestcentral'.
  • Google — the pricing tables embedded in the disks-and-images pricing page, Frankfurt column.
  • OVHcloud — the public order catalogue, /order/catalog/public/cloud for the DE subsidiary. Exoscale — the pricing API its own price page consumes. STACKIT — price list PDF v1.0.43.
  • Vendor pricing moves without notice: re-run the collection before reusing a reading more than a month old.
