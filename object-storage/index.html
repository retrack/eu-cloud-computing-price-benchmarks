Who states their unit, and who doesn't

  • Binary, stated in writing — AWS (“1 GB is 2³⁰ bytes”), Azure, Google (“JEDEC binary gigabytes”), Exoscale (billed per GiB-hour), OVHcloud (GiB column), Wasabi (1 TB = 1,024 GB).
  • Not stated — Hetzner, Backblaze, Scaleway, IONOS, Cloudflare, Infomaniak, STACKIT. Those rows carry a marker and follow the selector at the top of the page. The gap between the two assumptions is 7.4 %.

Regions are not interchangeable

  • OVHcloud: the 3-AZ multi-zone offer exists only in Paris and Milan. In Frankfurt/Limburg it is single-zone — that is the row to compare against Frankfurt.
  • Backblaze: the European region is Amsterdam, not Frankfurt.
  • Infomaniak: Switzerland. STACKIT: Germany South. Exoscale: 8 zones across CH/DE/AT/BG/HR.
  • Azure Hot LRS is single-datacentre: the honest comparison against S3 Standard is Hot ZRS.

Documented gaps, left as gaps

  • STACKIT publishes neither traffic nor request pricing. Its scenario cost is therefore a floor, marked ≥.
  • T Cloud Public (formerly Open Telekom Cloud) publishes no storage price at all: calculator only.
  • IONOS shows €0.007/GB/30 d on the German page and $0.00487/GB/30 d on the US page — a ~44 % gap FX does not explain.
  • Wasabi publishes no rate card for its commitment discounts: the “max discount” on the current chart cannot be sourced.
  • Exoscale charges no requests, but nowhere states so explicitly.

Scenario assumptions

  • Retrieval fees apply to the month's egress volume: pulling 10 TiB out of a cold class means restoring it first.
  • Storage and egress volume tiers are applied tier by tier, not at an average rate.
  • Minimum storage durations are not monetised — that needs a churn rate specific to each workload. The column states them.
  • Minimum billable object sizes (128 KiB at Exoscale and AWS, 64 KB at Hetzner, 4 KB at Wasabi) weigh heavily on a small-object estate and are likewise not monetised.
  • Prices exclude VAT, negotiated discounts and commitment terms.

How the rates were collected

AWS, Azure, Google, Hetzner and Exoscale render their prices in JavaScript: values were read from the rendered DOM with the region explicitly selected, or straight from the price feeds those pages consume (AWS meteredUnitMaps files, the Azure Retail Prices API, Hetzner's price API). Two cautions:

  • Hetzner's launch press release still shows €4.99/month and €0.0067/TB·h; its price API — the one feeding the product page — returns €6.49 and €0.0087/TB·h. The latter is what is used here.
  • Vendor pricing moves without notice: re-run the collection before reusing a reading more than a month old.
